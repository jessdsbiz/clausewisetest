# Product Requirements Document

## 1. Project Overview
**Project:** ClauseWise SG
**Challenge Track:** Law
**Build Format:** Deployable web application (mobile-first, installable on phones as a PWA)

## 2. Problem Statement
Job seekers, employees, tenants, freelancers, consumers and small business owners are often asked to sign contracts they don’t fully understand. Employment, rental, mobile phone, loan and business agreements are long, full of legal terms like indemnity, liability, non-compete, probation and automatic renewal, and the important conditions are easy to miss. So people overlook penalties, restrictive clauses, hidden fees, deposit deductions, early termination charges and termination conditions. A job seeker might agree to a broad non-compete without knowing it, or accept being let go at the end of probation without the notice pay their contract gives them. A tenant might not understand when their deposit can be kept, and a phone customer might not realise their plan locks them in for 12 or 24 months with a fee to leave early. Many also can’t spot scam signs, like being asked to pay a fee to start a job or to send money to a personal account. Asking a lawyer feels expensive, slow or intimidating, especially when you don’t know what to ask. And text-heavy contracts are even harder for people with limited legal knowledge, language barriers or different learning styles. People need an easier way to understand what they’re signing, see the risks and decide before it’s too late.

### Evidence / Why this matters
Contract misunderstandings often end up as disputes. MOM’s Employment Standards Report 2025 recorded 10,801 salary claims and 2,168 wrongful dismissal claims in 2025, the highest since 2019. Many of these come down to what the contract says about pay, notice and termination.

Real cases show that the contract decides who keeps the money. In 2026, a supporting officer at a Singapore law firm was told two days before his probation ended that he wouldn’t be kept on, and was offered only 7 days’ “goodwill” pay. Because his contract required one month’s notice during probation, he claimed and won about S$2,900 at the Employment Claims Tribunal, a decision upheld on appeal (CNA, Aug 2026). In another 2026 case, an employee who left without serving his six-month notice period was ordered to pay his former employer S$36,000 plus interest (Aerospace Solutions v Low Eng Wah [2026] SGMC 89). Same type of clause, opposite results: the difference is understanding what was signed. Most workers in the first case would have taken the 7 days and lost the rest.

Tenants face the same problem. In a 2026 Small Claims Tribunal case, an HDB landlord withheld two tenants’ $800 deposits, and the magistrate found none of the landlord’s reasons justified keeping them.

Phone and subscription contracts are a growing issue too. Telecommunications complaints to the Consumers Association of Singapore (CASE) rose from 529 in 2023 to 710 in 2024, and telcos were the top sector for complaints about hard-to-cancel subscriptions in 2024 and 2025. In one reported case, a customer picked a “no contract” plan online but was later told he was on a 12-month contract and would have to pay early termination charges to leave.

Singapore already has MOM, the Tripartite Alliance for Dispute Management (TADM), CASE and Pro Bono SG to help people with employment, consumer and legal problems, which shows people need help understanding their rights and obligations, but this help usually comes after something has gone wrong. Scams are a risk too: the Singapore Police Force’s Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025 recorded 41,974 scam cases and $913.1 million lost in 2025, and job scams were the third-biggest scam type by money lost.

Common problem areas include probation and termination conditions, salary disputes, rental deposits, early termination and cancellation fees, and unclear responsibilities. Many people also don’t read terms and conditions at all; “does anyone actually read the terms and conditions?” is a common question online (e.g. Reddit r/NoStupidQuestions). Terms like indemnity, liability, non-compete, probation and automatic renewal need to be explained in everyday language so anyone can understand them.

## 3. Target User
**Primary user:** Job seeker, young worker, tenant or phone plan customer in Singapore (including fresh graduates and migrant workers) who has been given a contract to sign, has no legal background, and may be more comfortable in Chinese, Malay, Tamil or another language than legal English
**Context:** Has just received a job offer, tenancy agreement, mobile phone plan or loan document, often told to sign within a day or two (or on the spot at a phone shop), reading it alone on their phone with no one who knows the law to ask
**Goal:** Understand what they’re agreeing to, spot anything risky or suspicious, and sign (or say no) with confidence
**Pain points:** Contracts are long and full of legal terms; important conditions are buried in the small print; they don’t know what questions to ask; a lawyer feels too expensive or intimidating for “just” a job offer, lease or phone plan; probation and notice terms are easy to misread; phone plans hide the contract length, early termination fee and cancellation window in the terms and conditions; scam contracts look real; and the contract is usually only in English

## 4. Current Journey
- User gets a long contract (e.g. a 15-page job offer, a tenancy agreement or a 24-month phone plan) and is told to sign quickly, sometimes within 24 hours or on the spot
- They don’t understand terms like indemnity, non-compete, probation or automatic renewal, don’t know who to ask, and seeing a lawyer feels costly, so they skim it or sign without reading
- Problems show up later (a long notice period, being let go at the end of probation without notice pay, deposit deductions, an early termination fee, a phone plan that can only be cancelled in a narrow window, a restrictive clause, or a fake job offer that asks for an upfront fee) when it’s too late, leaving them with lost money or a dispute

## 5. Ideal AI-Enabled Journey
- Before signing, user taps “Scan a contract”, uploads the PDF or snaps a photo of each page (hiding their NRIC), and picks the language. They don’t need to type anything: ClauseWise SG puts the pages in order, flags a blurry or missing page, and detects the contract type and who it is with (“Detected: Job offer from BrightPath Solutions · Change”), so the user just taps to confirm. They can say or type a few words about their situation in “Tell us about you” (e.g. “My first full-time job. I want to study part-time next year.”)
- ClauseWise SG reads it and shows a plain-words summary (pay, hours, probation, notice, contract length, fees, cancellation window and key dates), with each point tagged to its clause and the points that matter most for this user shown first
- The risks and scam check shows any warning signs with a priority level and the source clause (e.g. “You must pay S$350 before you start”, Clause 5.3, or “Early termination fee stays at S$500 even near the end of your plan”, Clause 8.1), with a “Why this matters for you” line where the user’s situation makes a warning more relevant. The user marks each warning “I’ll ask about this”, “I’m OK with this” or “This looks wrong”
- User picks a confusing clause and sees it as a 4-panel comic, or listens to it read aloud, with the original words shown underneath
- User asks the AI chatbot follow-up questions and gets “Your plan before you sign”: 3–6 steps, each linked to a clause and a place to get help (MOM, TADM, Pro Bono SG, ScamShield 1799, ACRA)
- On the “You decide” screen, the user checks and edits an AI-drafted message in English to the employer or landlord (with a plain-words version in their own language underneath), sends it themselves if they want to, and chooses Sign, Negotiate first, Get expert help or Walk away. ClauseWise SG never chooses for them and never sends anything

## 6. Proposed Solution
**One-line value proposition:** ClauseWise SG reads a contract before you sign it (a job offer, tenancy, phone plan or loan) and explains it in plain words, as a comic or out loud in your language. It also flags possible scam signs and risky terms, and lets you ask an AI chatbot about any clause.

The user opens ClauseWise SG and taps “Scan a contract”, then uploads a PDF or takes photos of each page (up to 20, or several picked from the gallery at once), which they can retake, delete or reorder. AI reads every page, puts the pages in order, and asks instead of guessing when a page is blurry, missing or repeated. They choose how to explain it (Plain words, Comic or Audio) and the language (English, 中文, Melayu, தமிழ், and more). The contract type (Job offer, Rental, Phone / Subscription, Loan, Business or Other) is detected and pre-filled, so the user only taps to confirm or change it, and they can speak instead of typing in “Tell us about you” and the chatbot. The contract overview opens with four tabs. Summary shows the key points in plain words (e.g. “Even during probation, they must give you 1 month’s notice or pay you for it” or “This plan lasts 24 months. To cancel, tell them in the last 7 days before it ends”), each tagged to its clause, plus key dates like “Reply within 24 hrs”. Risks shows a scam check banner (e.g. “4 possible scam signs. Don’t pay anything yet.”), each sign with its priority and source, and buttons to look up the company on ACRA, call the ScamShield Helpline (1799), report a scam, or contact CASE for phone and subscription issues. Comic lets the user pick a clause and see it as a 4-panel story, with the original words and any assumptions under each panel. Ask opens the AI chatbot, which answers questions about this contract and quotes the clause, with an option to request a real expert. If the user wrote a few words in the optional “Tell us about you” box when scanning, Summary puts what matters most for them first, warnings explain “Why this matters for you”, and a “Made for you” banner jumps to “Your plan before you sign”: 3–6 steps at the end of the Summary tab, each linked to a clause and a place to get help (MOM, TADM, Pro Bono SG, ScamShield 1799, ACRA or CASE). On each warning the user picks “I’ll ask about this”, “I’m OK with this” or “This looks wrong”. Finally, the “You decide” screen turns the “I’ll ask about this” warnings into a polite AI-drafted message in English to the employer, landlord or telco, with a plain-words version in the user’s language underneath, which they check, edit and send themselves. It then offers Sign, Negotiate first, Get expert help or Walk away, with nothing pre-selected and no recommendation. Behind the scenes, two different AIs check each other: once Claude has written the summary, warnings and plan, an NVIDIA model checks each one against its clause and looks for risky clauses that were missed; Claude fixes what it finds, and a badge (“Double-checked by a second AI · 2 fixed”) shows what changed. Every screen says ClauseWise SG explains contracts and isn’t legal advice.

### Differentiation
Unlike a general AI chatbot or a plain document summariser, ClauseWise SG shows people what a clause could mean in real life through short comics, so they see it instead of just reading about it. Users choose how they want it explained (plain words, comic or audio) and in which language. Every explanation, warning and comic panel links back to the exact clause, so users can check it themselves. It’s built for Singapore: scam signs based on SPF and ScamShield advice, phone plan checks based on IMDA’s Contract Period and Early Termination Guidelines, and links to ACRA, MOM, TADM, CASE and Pro Bono SG for next steps. It’s also frugal: free browser read-aloud and reusable comic templates keep costs low, so basic checks can stay free for students and low-income workers.

## 7. AI Design
### AI capabilities
- Text / LLM
- Image / Vision
- Knowledge search / RAG
- Image generation
- Speech / voice

### Why AI is appropriate
Contracts come as long PDFs or phone photos, every company words things differently, and the meaning depends on context. A fixed keyword list can’t read a scanned contract, explain what a notice or probation clause means for this person, notice that the payee is a personal name instead of the company, work out whether a phone plan’s early termination fee goes down over time, or turn a clause into a scene someone can picture. AI can read the whole document, find the terms that matter, explain them in the user’s language and answer follow-up questions. It is grounded in the contract’s own text and a small set of official Singapore sources (MOM, SPF/ScamShield, ACRA, CASE and IMDA), so every answer points back to a real clause instead of being made up.

### AI tasks
Reads the uploaded PDF or each photo page (vision), puts photo pages in order using page numbers, headings and clause numbers, flags pages that are hard to read, missing or repeated, and splits the whole contract into numbered clauses; detects the contract type, the document’s language, the other party and key dates so the user only confirms them; pulls out pay, hours, probation, notice period, contract length, fees, deposits, early termination charges, cancellation window, non-compete, renewal, payment details and dates; checks these against a list of warning signs from SPF/ScamShield, MOM and IMDA guidance (e.g. paying to start a job, payment to a personal account, pressure to sign within 24 hours, no UEN or office address, a very broad non-compete, a probation clause with no notice period, auto-renewal onto a pricier plan, a large or fixed early termination fee that doesn’t go down over time, a very short cancellation window, a phone contract longer than 24 months) and labels each High, Medium or Info with the clause it came from; rewrites clauses in plain words in the chosen language; writes a 4-panel comic storyboard for a chosen clause, with captions, stated assumptions and the source clause, then illustrates it; answers chatbot questions using only the contract and checked sources; reads the user’s own description of their situation (no fixed filters or rules) and uses it to reorder the summary, add “Why this matters for you” lines to warnings and build “Your plan before you sign” (3–6 steps, each linked to a clause and a help source); drafts a polite message in English about the warnings the user wants to ask about, with a plain-words version in their language, for them to check, edit and send themselves; and has a second AI from another company (NVIDIA) check every summary point, warning and plan step against its clause and list risky clauses that were missed, so Claude can fix them.

## 8. MVP Scope
### Must Have

- Comic explanation: turns a chosen clause into a 4-panel comic with the original words and assumptions shown

## 9. Functional Requirements
- FR1: A user can upload a PDF or take a photo of a contract, sees a reminder to hide their NRIC first, and can delete the upload at any time.
- FR2: The Summary tab shows pay, hours, probation, notice, contract length, fees and key dates in plain words, each tagged with its clause number.
- FR3: The Risks tab lists warning signs labelled High, Medium or Info with the source clause, plus working ACRA, ScamShield 1799 and CASE links. It never says a contract is “safe”, “illegal” or “a scam”.
- FR4: The Comic tab turns a chosen clause into a 4-panel comic showing the original clause text, stated assumptions and the label “AI-generated example scene”.
- FR5: The Ask tab answers questions using only the uploaded contract, quotes the clause it used, and says “I’m not sure, ask an expert” when it can’t find the answer.
- FR6: The user can switch between plain words, comic and audio, in English, Chinese, Malay and Tamil.
- FR7: “Your situation”: the scan screen has an optional “Tell us about you” text box. The AI reads the user’s own words (no fixed filters or rules) and uses them to reorder the summary, adjust risk priorities with a “Why this matters for you” line, and create “Your plan before you sign”: 3–6 steps, each linked to a clause and a help source such as MOM, TADM, Pro Bono SG, ScamShield 1799 or ACRA.
- FR8: “You decide”: on each AI warning the user picks “I’ll ask about this”, “I’m OK with this” or “This looks wrong”. A final “You decide” screen shows an AI-drafted, editable message in English to the employer or landlord, labelled “AI draft – check and edit before sending”, with a plain-words version in the user’s chosen language underneath, and four options: Sign / Negotiate first / Get expert help / Walk away. Nothing is pre-selected, there is no recommendation, and the app never sends anything itself.
- FR9: “No more typing”: the user can use ClauseWise SG without typing. They can photograph a contract page by page (up to 20 pages, or pick several from the gallery) and retake, delete or reorder pages. AI (not fixed lists or keyword rules) reads each page, puts the pages in order, and flags a page that is hard to read, a page that looks missing or the same page twice instead of guessing. The pages become one contract with numbered clauses, the same as a PDF. On upload, AI detects the contract type, the document’s language, the other party and key dates, and pre-fills them for the user to confirm. A mic button lets the user speak in “Tell us about you” and the chatbot, in their chosen language, and check the words before sending.
- FR10: “Make it fail”: two different AIs check each other. After Claude writes the summary, warnings and plan (and they pass the existing code checks), an NVIDIA model checks every item against its clause text (does it match, is anything invented, changed or missing?) and lists risky clauses no warning covers. Claude rewrites anything marked wrong using only the clause text, and NVIDIA checks the fix once more; if it still fails, the app shows the clause itself with “We couldn’t explain this one clearly. Read the clause or ask an expert.” A scam-sign warning is never dropped. Missed risks become warnings marked “Found on second check”. If NVIDIA takes over 15 seconds or fails, Claude does the check and the app says so. A badge on the Summary and Risks tabs (“Double-checked by a second AI · 2 fixed” or “· all clear”) shows which AI checked, what was wrong, what it became and the clause. With ?demo=fail in the address, two mistakes are added on purpose (the notice period changed from 30 to 3 days and the S$350 fee warning removed), clearly labelled “Demo mode: mistakes added on purpose”, so judges can watch them being caught and fixed.

## 10. Technical Approach
**Likely stack:** Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (mobile-first, purple ClauseWise SG branding), set up as a PWA so it installs on phones. pdf.js to read PDFs. The Claude API (called through Netlify Functions, with the API key kept in .env) reads photos and writes the summaries, risk flags, the chatbot answers, “Your plan before you sign”, translations and comic storyboards, using Claude Haiku so every step answers within the 30-second function limit (the risk check has its own model setting). NVIDIA Build models stay as a backup, switched on with one setting in .env, and an NVIDIA model (Nemotron Ultra) is the second AI that double-checks Claude’s work, with Claude Haiku as its backup. Reusable illustrated templates for comic panels. JavaScript rules for common scam signs and phone plan terms, and a small knowledge base of MOM, TADM, ScamShield, ACRA, CASE and IMDA (Contract Period and Early Termination Guidelines) guidance for grounding. Browser Web Speech API for audio and voice input, Supabase for storage, GitHub for version control, and Netlify hosting.
**Delivery format:** Deployable web application (mobile-first PWA)

## 11. Responsible AI & Risk Controls
- Human review / approval
- Privacy / data minimisation
- Source citation / grounding
- Hallucination mitigation
- AI-generated media labelling

### Primary risk and mitigation
The biggest risk is the AI getting a clause wrong (changing its meaning or missing an exception), a comic inventing an obligation, or the user treating ClauseWise SG as legal advice or a “safe to sign” stamp. Mitigation: every explanation, warning and comic panel links to the exact original clause so users can check it (grounding). Comics state their assumptions (e.g. “Assumed: Mei has passed probation”) and are labelled “AI-generated example scene”. Labels show review priority only; ClauseWise SG never says a contract is safe, illegal or a scam. Where a check is based on guidelines rather than law (e.g. IMDA’s advisory guidelines on phone contracts), it says “worth asking about”, not “this is not allowed”. The chatbot only answers from the contract and checked sources, and says “I’m not sure, ask an expert” when it can’t find the answer (hallucination controls). Every screen says “not legal advice”, and for big decisions the app suggests a qualified professional and links to TADM, Pro Bono SG, CASE and ScamShield (human review). The user stays in charge of every decision: they mark each AI warning “I’ll ask about this”, “I’m OK with this” or “This looks wrong”, check and edit the AI-drafted message (labelled “AI draft – check and edit before sending”) and send it themselves, since ClauseWise SG never sends anything, and make the final choice on the “You decide” screen, where nothing is pre-selected and the app makes no recommendation (human review). Two different AIs check each other: a second AI from another company checks each explanation against its clause, anything it finds wrong is rewritten and checked again, anything still not right is replaced by the clause itself, a scam-sign warning is never removed, and the badge shows the user exactly what was changed (hallucination mitigation). “Your situation” only changes order and emphasis: it never hides a warning or makes a scam sign look less serious, and every “Why this matters for you” line and plan step links to a clause like any other output (grounding). Users are reminded to hide their NRIC before scanning, every photo page and what they type or say in “Tell us about you” or the chatbot is masked the same way as the contract text, photos are only kept in the browser until their text has been read, the detected contract type is only a suggestion the user confirms or changes (human review), the demo uses fictional contracts only, uploads can be deleted anytime, and personal details are not stored (privacy).

## 12. Success & Validation
**Expected impact:** Help people understand what they’re signing and spot risky terms and scam signs before they sign, so fewer end up losing money or in disputes. This supports SDG 8 (decent work), SDG 10 (reduced inequalities) and SDG 16 (access to justice).
**Primary metric:** In user testing, increase the share of key terms and planted warning signs users correctly identify in a sample contract, from an estimated 40% reading alone to over 80% with ClauseWise SG
**Secondary metric:** Users understand a chosen clause within 60 seconds, and at least 4 in 5 test users say the comic or plain-words view made the contract easier to understand
**Prototype test:** Use three fictional contracts: an 8-page job offer (BrightPath Solutions) with a probation clause requiring one month’s notice, a tenancy agreement, and a 24-month phone plan (SwiftMobile) with a fixed early termination fee, a 7-day cancellation window and auto-renewal onto a pricier plan. Each has 6 key terms and 4 planted scam or risk signs. Give them to 10–12 participants (students, young workers, and at least two who prefer Chinese, Malay or Tamil). Half read the contract on their own for 5 minutes; the other half use ClauseWise SG. Both groups answer the same 8-question quiz. Compare scores, time taken and how confident they feel, and note which “Explain it as” mode they used. Also ask our mentor or a law lecturer to check the AI’s explanations of 20 clauses for accuracy.

## 14. Definition of Done
- A user can complete the core journey from upload (with or without “Tell us about you”) to summary, risk check, comic, chatbot answer, “Your plan before you sign” and the “You decide” screen without the demo breaking.
- AI performs the intended task meaningfully and reliably enough for a live demo.
- At least the Must Have features are working, tested on the fictional BrightPath job offer, including with two different “Tell us about you” texts that give a different summary order, “Why this matters for you” lines and plan, with no warning removed.
- “Make it fail” is tested on BrightPath normally and with ?demo=fail, in English and Chinese, and with NVIDIA switched off: the planted mistakes are caught and fixed, and the badge shows which AI checked and what changed.
- “No more typing” is tested on the BrightPath PDF and on photos of BrightPath pages taken out of order, with one page left out, and with one blurry photo: the pages are put in order, the missing page and the blurry photo are flagged, and the summary works on the combined contract.
- Responsible AI safeguards are visible where relevant, including the “not legal advice”, “warning signs, not a final verdict” and “AI draft – check and edit before sending” notices, and a “You decide” screen with nothing pre-selected, no recommendation and no way for the app to send the message itself.
- The application is responsive, installable on a phone and demo-ready.
- The team can explain the architecture, AI role, limitations and expected impact.
